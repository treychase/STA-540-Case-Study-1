# STA 540 Case-Study 1

# Study Overview

## Background/Motivation

Advertisements on platforms such as social media sites, dating apps, and search engines are common ways for promotions to reach consumers. However, it remains unclear which platform is most efficient in promoting home HIV self-testing among minority men who have sex with men (MSM), given that users of various platforms may have different characteristics that impact their readiness for HIV testing. While these platforms have been widely adopted for HIV prevention recruitment, there is a lack of head-to-head comparisons evaluating their relative effectiveness in promoting HIV self-testing and facilitating test kit orders.

## Primary Objective

To compare the relative effectiveness of three types of web-based platforms in promoting HIV self-testing among young (18-30 years old) minority (Black or Latinx) MSM at increased risk of HIV infection:
- **Social media sites** (Facebook, Instagram, Twitter)
- **Dating apps** (Grindr, Jack'D, Hornet)
- **Information search sites** (Google, Bing, Yahoo)

Test kit order rates are the primary measurement of effectiveness of each platform type.

## Secondary Objectives

1. To evaluate the association between HIV self-test kit ordering and key moderating variables, including:
   - Substance use patterns
   - Psychological readiness to test (based on the transtheoretical model/stages of change)
   - Attitudes toward HIV testing and treatment
   - HIV-related stigma
   - Medical mistrust

2. To assess differences in participant characteristics between those who ordered versus those who did not order an HIV test kit

3. To record and analyze advertisement campaign performance metrics (reach, engagement, and cost-effectiveness) across different platforms
